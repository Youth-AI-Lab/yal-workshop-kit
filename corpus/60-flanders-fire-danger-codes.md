# Flanders (Antwerp): the four fire danger colour codes
Source: Agentschap voor Natuur en Bos (ANB, Flemish Agency for Nature and Forests), page "Brandpreventie in natuur- en bosgebieden op basis van kleurcodes", https://natuurenbos.be/brandpreventie-natuur-en-bosgebieden-op-basis-van-kleurcodes, no publication date shown, Dutch
Last checked: 2026-10-19 by Claude (draft — needs human verification)

In Flanders, the Agency for Nature and Forests (ANB) publishes a fire danger code for its nature and forest areas.
The fire-danger season starts on 1 March and ends on 30 September.
The danger is assessed weekly to daily.
It uses the Fire Weather Index and other indices from EFFIS, the European Forest Fire Information System.
It also uses forecasts from the Royal Meteorological Institute of Belgium (KMI).
There are four colour codes: green, yellow, orange and red.
The codes are set per province and apply to all ANB nature and forest areas in that province.
Green means little danger; the fire brigade responds at normal strength.
Yellow means danger; do not smoke in nature and forest areas and do not make fire.
Orange means high danger; do not leave children unsupervised in the area.
Red means acute high danger; going into nature and forest areas is discouraged.
At red, fire towers are staffed permanently during the day, unless the ANB and the fire services agree otherwise because of local conditions.
Heathland and coniferous forests are the most fire-sensitive areas in Flanders.
In nature and forest areas, making fire or throwing cigarette butts on the ground is always forbidden.
