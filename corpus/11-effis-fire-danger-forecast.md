# EFFIS fire danger forecast and the Fire Weather Index
Source: European Forest Fire Information System (EFFIS, Copernicus Emergency Management Service / JRC), page "Fire Danger Forecast" (technical background), https://forest-fire.emergency.copernicus.eu/about-effis/technical-background/fire-danger-forecast, no publication date shown, English
Last checked: 2026-10-19 by Claude (draft — needs human verification)

In 2007, the EFFIS network adopted the Canadian Forest Fire Weather Index (FWI) System.
It is used to assess fire danger in the same way across Europe.
This choice followed a 5-year test of different national fire danger indices.
EFFIS computes the FWI from two weather models.
The ECMWF model (8 km) gives forecasts from 1 to 9 days.
The Météo-France model (10 km) gives forecasts up to 3 days.
The FWI is mapped in 6 danger classes.
The class table gives these FWI values: Low (below 11.2), Moderate (11.2–21.3), High (21.3–38.0), Very High (38.0–50.0), Extreme (50.0–70.0), Very Extreme (above 70.0).
The text of the page calls the second class "medium"; its table calls it "Moderate".
The "Very Extreme" class was introduced in June 2021.
It was added to distinguish danger levels within large Mediterranean areas that were classified "Extreme" in the summer months.
The classes are the same for all countries.
EFFIS also publishes two indicators that compare the FWI with about 40 years of data: the ranking (percentiles) and the anomaly.
