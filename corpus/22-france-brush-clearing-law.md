# France: the legal obligation to clear brush (OLD)
Source: Service-Public (Direction de l'information légale et administrative, Premier ministre), "Dans quel cas le débroussaillement est-il obligatoire ?", https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F33298, "Vérifié le 07 janvier 2025", French
Last checked: 2026-10-19 by Claude (draft — needs human verification)

In France, the legal obligation to clear brush is called "obligation légale de débroussaillement" (OLD).
It applies to land less than 200 metres from forests, heathland, maquis or garrigue classified as at risk of fire.
The owner is responsible for clearing brush around their building.
A tenant may do the work if the lease says so, but the owner keeps the criminal responsibility.
Clearing must cover at least 50 metres around the house and other installations.
This zone can extend onto a neighbour's plot.
Along access roads, clearing covers up to 10 metres on each side.
In urban zones of a local urban plan (PLU), the whole plot must also be cleared.
The precise rules are set by a prefectoral order in each département.
Since 1 January 2025, sellers and landlords must tell buyers or tenants if the property is subject to the OLD.
The mayor checks the obligation and can order the work, with a penalty of up to 100 € per day of delay.
There can be an administrative fine of up to 50 € per square metre not cleared.
The insurance excess can also be increased, up to 5 000 €.
